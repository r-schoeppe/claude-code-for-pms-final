## Evidence by fire

**F1 Four cut off (VERIFIED counts, MODELLED scores).**
- Weekly pings to the four: 49, 48, 48, 50, 50, 49, 43, 16, 6, 3 (weeks from 29 Jun). The other twelve: 123 to 126, then 134, 142, 156, 162. `00-rook/data/callout-history.csv`.
- Jobs taken a day, before to after: Farlight 1.25 to 0.08, The Undertow 1.32 to 0.12, Meteor Mite 1.09 to 0.12, Vesper 1.59 to 0.19.
- Their miss rate after 12 Aug is 59% (33 of 56), against 13.9% for everyone else, and 2.9% for the four before.
- Replaying the real scoring rule over real outcomes puts exactly these four at 0.00 and the other twelve at 0.72 or higher. `00-rook/feedback/score-replay-real-pings.py`.
- The handlers' words: "starting to wonder if im still even in the system" (tickets 3120 and 3121).
- Business angle: pricing is per active responder, so a responder who stops getting work is a retention risk. Two export-history requests (3051, 3106) are ambiguous; they say "for our own records" and are not evidence of leaving.

**F2 Missed pings (VERIFIED).**
- Daily misses were 0 or 1 a day until 11 Aug, then 7, 12, 8, 6, 4 on 12 to 16 Aug. They stay at 2 to 7 a day through 6 Sep, against roughly 0.3 a day before.
- It is not a time-of-day effect: misses went from 1.6% to 18.4% in the morning, 1.8% to 17.5% in the afternoon and 2.5% to 19.0% in the evening.
- The healthy twelve alone miss 15.7%, 13.4%, 12.8%, 11.1% in the four weeks from 10 Aug, against 1.5% to 2.4% before. So this is not only the four.
- The daily dip after 13 Aug is partly composition: the four, who miss most, stopped being pinged.
- The shorter wait did not speed assignment. Time from callout to the ping that was taken: median 17 s before, 19 s after; 90th percentile 41 s before, 78 s after. This is a proxy for time-to-accept; the database has no response-time field.

## Challenging what you have been told

| Claim | Who | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "Mostly seasonal; August is always soft" | Priya, 19 Aug and handover | **Half right.** Callouts a week fell about 12% (137 to 144 before; 110, 121, 124, 127 after), so demand did dip. But misses jumped on release day, and four responders collapsed. Neither is seasonal. No prior-year data exists, so even the demand dip cannot be called seasonal. |
| "Should settle once September starts" | Engineering manager, 26 Aug | **Not yet shown.** The 31 Aug week still had 12.7% missed and 25 tickets. Callouts nobody took did improve. |
| "Right change to make; reverting trades one angry group for another" | Priya | **Partly.** Wide-area responders gained 10% to 28% more jobs a day, and pings to responders from another area are now taken (86 of 241) where before they never were (0 of 186). But the share of callouts whose first ping went to a home-area responder fell from 80% to 61%, and the cost fell on the four. Timeout and miss penalty can be changed without reverting the weights. |
| "Phone never goes off" is a mystery | Support lead | **Mostly explained.** For the four, pings stopped coming (ranking). But 59% of the few they did get were missed, so delivery problems are not ruled out. |
| Ambrose: near-miss "two weeks ago" | Interview, 2 Sep | **Wrong date.** Ticket 3043 ("yesterday evening", filed 13 Aug) and the ping log (callout 40987, 12 Aug 19:03, Captain Vantage missed) agree on release day. The home responder, Nightwell, was pinged last, 163 s after the callout. |
| Halloran: maintenance scheduling "improved" | Interview, 5 Sep | **Not a contradiction.** He spoke about Bulwark. The marathon tickets are about Falkirk and Ironvale. One handler's responder against two others. |
| Aunt Dot: quiet weeks and fast-vanishing ones | Interview, 3 Sep | **Prompted.** The interviewer asked about quiet weeks and she answered "now that you mention it". The data still backs it for Vesper (1.59 to 0.19 jobs a day). |

Weaknesses of the evidence:
- The four interviews were for console redesign, not 4.2. Only two of the four handlers' responders are among the four cut off (Kip's Meteor Mite, Aunt Dot's Vesper). Nobody was interviewed for Farlight, The Undertow, Ashgrove or Halfmoon, who filed the most tickets.
- "Home area" is not "nearest". Responders move, so the first-ping-home fall does not prove the new weights misfire.
- The score replay is a model. Farlight was still asked first at scores as low as 0.28, so the score alone does not explain being unreachable. Travel times and the three score components are not in the data.
- The simulation showed the 4.1 and 4.2 weights give almost the same number of low-ranked responders. It did not reproduce the real starvation.
- The data stops at 7 Sep and the system date is 15 Oct. Anything after 7 Sep is not visible; ask for a refreshed extract before acting on "current".

## Challenging the planned actions

**Do-this-week stopgap: reset the four to 0.5.** Reasonable, but test it properly.
- A reset leaves them about 0.12 below healthy colleagues (0.25 weight times roughly 0.5), worth about 9 minutes of travel. It may not put them back in rotation.
- It does not stop the cause: one more miss costs 0.12 again.
- Better design: reset two of the four and keep two as a comparison for seven days. Agree the success measure first, for example at least one ping a day each.

**Diagnostics, owner the staff engineer via the engineering manager:**
(a) the three score components for Farlight and her three neighbours on callouts 40985 and 41416; (b) push-delivery logs for the four on 12 and 13 Aug; (c) travel times from the four to their home areas; (d) whether the 4.2 deploy reset the in-memory `_scores`; and the engineering manager's 14 Aug question about decliners.
- Add (e): the same score components for callouts 40987 and 40997, where the home responder was pinged last.
- Add (f): an ops check on whether the 49 unserved callouts were handled another way.

**Cross-team guard.** Do not change the shape of what `availability.current_record()` returns; Rook Supply reads it. Tell Supply about the marathon tickets.

**Ranked fixes (INFERRED):**
1. Stop treating a miss like a refusal.
2. Let stuck scores recover, and persist them.
3. Pilot a 75 to 90 s wait.
4. Guarantee a fair share of pings.
5. Revert the weights (lowest: the simulation shows they barely matter).
